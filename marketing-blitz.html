Competitor Recon

What the other kids' money books are running, and what we take from each. None of them have a story kids re-read; all of them have distribution tricks worth stealing.

Sammy Rabbit (Sam Renick) — the school-visit machine

Renick has read, sung, and skit-ed his rabbit into schools across 30 states and 7 countries, reaching 250,000+ students. The trick isn't the rabbit; it's who pays. Credit unions and banks sponsor the visits and buy the books, because "we taught local kids to save" is exactly what their community-reinvestment reports need.

Steal thisPitch credit unions as sponsors, not customers. Our CRA memo in grant-in-a-box is already written for this. 25 copies = $499.75 puts a branch's name inside every folder that goes home. Template below.

Danny Dollar Academy (Ty Allan Jackson) — the book that became a program

Jackson turned one book into "Danny Dollar Academy" — a named reading + finlit program that schools and state organizations adopt wholesale. A school can't put "we bought a book" in a grant report. It can put "we ran a program."

Steal thisStop saying "free downloads." Say Program in a Box everywhere, like it's a product with a name — because it is, and it's already on the site. Four 45-minute sessions, pre/post assessment, standards crosswalk. That's a program a principal can report on.

Grandpa's Fortune Fables (Will Rainey) — the audience before the book

Rainey built Blue Tree Savings as a parent newsletter and got bylines in the Financial Times before most people knew the book existed. The book rides an email list that shows up every week whether or not there's anything to sell.

Steal thisThe email signup is already on the site; give it a job. A 5-email "Money Talk Monday" series for parents (one concept per week, one thing to try at the store). And pitch the press with the hook Rainey can't use: attorney-dad writes the money book his kid would actually finish. Press kit is live at press-kit.html.

Greenlight & the kid-fintech crowd — content that catches the search

Greenlight, GoHenry, and Benjamin Talks publish "family vacation budget" and "souvenir allowance" articles every summer and own the search results for every worried parent. Their advice: give kids a $20 souvenir budget and let them feel the limit. That's page 25 of our book, with a plot attached.

Steal thisThe vacation campaign below is our version, with a boardwalk instead of a blog. Their tip is our story. Also: book-facts.html and llms.txt already feed the AI assistants parents ask; add the vacation angle to both so "book to stop vacation meltdowns" finds Clarence.

The indie kidlit August playbook — what every smart author does next month

The standard back-to-school moves across the category: classroom-set giveaways in August, ready-made activities teachers can print, introductions in educator Facebook groups, metadata cleanup on Amazon/Goodreads before the fall buying wave, and booking school visits in August for October.

Steal thisAll of it. Most authors improvise the "ready-made activity" part; ours is six print-PDFs deep. The week-by-week below runs the whole playbook with dates on it.
