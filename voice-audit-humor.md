You asked whether the letters sound like you. Straight answer, then the numbers, then a fix.

## The numbers

**17 humor beats across 669 letters. 0.03 per letter.** 654 of 669 have nothing.
And the few that exist are the *same* joke recycled — the kid resenting sales tax, in six
slight variations ("takes it personally," "has feelings about it," "ambushed by,"
"face-first into," "the hard way," "a personal betrayal"). One joke wearing six hats.

**Your vocabulary is barely in here.** Of 33 words characteristic of how you actually write —
*blurted, mumbled, kiddo, dude, buddy, recess, about-face, superhero, weapon, neon,
highlighter* — only **5** appear at all, and 104 of those hits are the word "register."

What the letters lean on instead, by frequency: *toolkit* (300), *financial* (232),
*teaches* (219), *educator* (152), *concepts* (79), *classroom* (60). That is grant-
application vocabulary. It is the exact register your own kill list was written to prevent,
and I walked into it.

## Where funny works, and where it gets you deleted

You said it yourself — funny doesn't work for some. Agreed. Here is the map I'd use.

### Tier A — full strength. Be actually funny. (~90 letters)
Peer authors · podcasters · columnists and press · children's librarians · the Rehoboth and
Lewes locals · bookstores · Tom Henske and the other engagement-driven posters · teachers and
principals who show personality in their own feed.

These people are in the business of being entertaining, or they read to children for a
living. A dry letter here is the mistake.

### Tier B — dry wit only. One turn of thought, no punchline. (~60)
Academics — Lusardi, Meszaros, Mahoney, Sticha · superintendents · association executive
directors · Mia Russell · Rachel Edoho-Eket.

DASA phone script.

The Suzann Knight letter is a compliance question in a queue. Humor there reads as someone

## What I'd change, beyond jokes

1. **Put the one-cent shoes in every Tier A letter.** It's your best thirty seconds and it's
   been sitting in the press kit unused.
2. **Retire "toolkit."** It appears 300 times. Say "the teacher stuff," "the classroom
   pack," "everything a teacher needs" — vary it like a person would.
3. **Vary the plot spine.** I fixed this in the Delaware set; the other 630 still march
   through the same six beats in the same order. A reader only sees one, but *you* have to
   believe it, and identical construction is how it starts sounding generated.
4. **Kill one hedge per letter.** "I'd be happy to," "if that's ever useful," "or am I
   overreaching" — one is charming, three is apologising for existing.
5. **Use "Page 22. Aisle Five."** It's the best line you own and it's in zero letters.

## Recommendation

Rewrite **Tier A only — roughly 90 letters** — with real humor, the one-cent shoes, and the
Crabby Guy where it's geographically apt. Leave Tier B lightly punched up. Do not touch
Tier C; deadpan is the correct register for a federal mailbox and I'd be making it worse.

Say go and I'll do Tier A. If you want, start with the 20 whales inside it so you can react
to a smaller batch before I commit to the rest.
