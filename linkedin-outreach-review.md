## 2. The two phrases on almost every message

**"I'm not trying to sell you anything."**

It's on roughly 99% of these. Three problems, in order of severity:

1. **It plants the idea it denies.** Nobody was thinking "sales pitch" until you
   said it. Now they are. It's the "no offense, but" of cold outreach.
2. **It isn't true, and they know it.** You're an author with a book at $19.99.
   Opening on a claim the recipient can disprove by clicking your profile costs
   you the thing you're trying to buy — credibility.
3. **At this volume it's a fingerprint.** This field is small and tightly
   networked. Tim Ranzetta, Yanely Espinal, and Ryan Wood all work at NGPF and
   all three get near-identical messages. Laura Levine, Joel Chrisler, and Gina
   Hutchinson are all Jump$tart. Lyn Haralson and Leslie Jones are both CFPB.
   They talk. The moment two of them compare, every message you sent reads as
   a mail merge — including the good ones.

Cut it. If you want to signal low-pressure, do it with the ask itself: a
question small enough to answer in one line is the only credible proof you
aren't selling.

**"Flip through a sample here, at least one chuckle guaranteed:"**

Same fingerprint problem, plus it's **106 characters** — 35% of a LinkedIn
connection note spent on a phrase that says nothing specific. "At least one
chuckle guaranteed" is also a promise you're making on their behalf before
they've read a word.

Just: *"Sample: [link]"*.

## 3. LinkedIn will not let you send these

**A connection request note is capped at 300 characters. Every plan. Spaces
count.** Measured against your actual messages:

| Message | Length | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Billy Hensley (Tier 1) | 599 | **299 over** |
| Perfect-Fit batch (~150 people) | 416 | **116 over** |
| Paula Pant | 470 | **170 over** |

So either these are already being truncated mid-sentence, or you're sending
them as InMail — which needs Premium or Sales Navigator, burns a credit each,
and comes with a monthly cap well under 365.

**The other ceiling:** roughly **100 connection invites per week** on a rolling
seven-day window before LinkedIn starts restricting the account. A 365-person
list is a minimum four-week campaign no matter what. Sent faster, you risk a
restriction that costs you the channel entirely.

This alone forces the list down. Might as well choose which ones to cut rather
than let LinkedIn choose for you.

## 5. The ask is wrong on nearly all of them

Almost every message asks for **"your read."** That's a request for thirty
minutes of a stranger's time, from a stranger, with no relationship. It's the
most expensive thing you could ask for and you're asking on first contact.

Worse, it leaves the valuable ask on the table. You never once ask the question
that has a one-word answer:

- Three Jump$tart people, and **not one message mentions the Clearinghouse** —
  the database you already qualify for, with published listing criteria you
  already meet.
- Two CFPB people, and **neither mentions Money as You Grow** by name.
- Ruben Rivera at CEE — no mention of a CEE resource listing.
- Nicolle Hood — no mention of her booklist.

"Would you read my book and tell me if it's good?" is a favor. "Does this meet
your listing criteria?" is a two-second yes/no that puts you in a database that
ranks. Ask for the second thing.

## 6. Voice — one direct violation of your own rules

**"It's not a book about saving money; it's about spending it wisely."**

That's the *"It's not X, it's Y"* construction. It's #11 on your own kill list
in CLAUDE.md — *"The most alive AI tell. Kill it every time."* It appears in
dozens of these.

The fix is already in your other variant, which is better because it's concrete:
*"Any book can teach a kid to save. The hard part is teaching them to spend."*
Use that one.

Two smaller things:

- **"I want it" / "is it worth it"** appear unquoted in most messages, so they
  read as a typo mid-sentence. Quote them or recast.
- **"I'd value your read"** closes something like 200 of these. Vary it or the
  repetition does the same work as the opener.

**Not a problem, and worth saying:** none of these leak the Clarence/Clearance
wordplay or explain the Wyze pun. Several get close to the orange-sign moment
and stop at the concept. That discipline held. Keep it.

## 10. What I'd actually do

1. **Kill the 11 broken merge fields and the 5 duplicates today.** Non-negotiable.
2. **Delete the two universal phrases** everywhere. That alone buys back 144
   characters per message.
3. **Cut the list to about 100.** Everything in section 4 goes. What's left
   should be people for whom you can name one specific thing — if you can't,
   they're not on the list.
4. **Change the ask** on every institutional contact from "your read" to the
   specific listing, database, or roundup you want into.
5. **Pace it at 25 a week.** Four weeks, well under the ceiling, and it gives you
   time to actually reply to the people who write back — which is the entire
   point and the thing a 365-blast makes impossible.
6. **Hand-write the top 20.** Section 9 covers nine of them.

One more thing worth saying plainly: 365 templated messages and 40 real ones
will produce roughly the same number of replies. The 40 won't cost you the
account, and they won't get compared over coffee at a Jump$tart conference.
