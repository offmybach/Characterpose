## Step 2 — InMail or follow-up (1818 / 1900)

**Subject:** `From a kindergarten classroom in Howard County to a K-5 book`

```
Dr. Edoho-Eket — congratulations on the ISTE+ASCD 20 to Watch. What caught me was not the award, it was what you wrote about it: that it started 21 years ago teaching five-year-olds in Howard County, and that those lessons still guide your leadership.

Those five-year-olds are my entire readership.

I wrote and illustrated a K-5 picture book that leaves saving out completely. It teaches spending, because that is the only money thing a six-year-old actually does. He reads the sale ad at the kitchen table, compares two robots on a shelf, takes the marked-down one on purpose, hands a coupon to the cashier, and then meets sales tax for the first time and takes it personally.

Two things make me think it belongs in your world rather than a bookstore's.

First, you have said your MAESP goal is centering joy in school leadership and reminding people why they chose education. My book is built on the same bet at kid level: they read it for the robot, and the money education sneaks in behind it. No worksheet appears at any point.

Second, the classroom materials are free and ungated. Four 45-minute lesson plans, a pre/post assessment with the answer key, a 23-row standards crosswalk against Jump$tart, Common Core math and ELA, CEE, and FDIC Money Smart. No login, no email capture, nothing consumable to reorder. A principal can hand a teacher the whole thing on a Friday.

I am not asking you to endorse it. I am asking whether an elementary principal would actually use it, and you have both the classroom memory and the building to answer that honestly.

I am in Baltimore, so coffee is easy if you would rather.

Concepts and specs: https://clarencegetsabargain.com/book-facts.html
Free toolkit: https://clarencegetsabargain.com/educator-toolkit.html
Sample: https://heyzine.com/flip-book/eeb1ef6cff.html
```

### Why it is built this way

- **Leads with her own words, not her titles.** She wrote that it started with five-year-olds in Howard County and that those lessons still guide her. Then: *"Those five-year-olds are my entire readership."* That is the whole letter in one turn.
- **Congratulates the award, then immediately says the award was not the interesting part.** Flattery that goes somewhere.
- **Uses "centering joy" back at her,** because it is her language and it happens to be true of the book.
- **The ask is deliberately small.** Not an endorsement. Whether a principal would actually use it. She can answer that in two lines.
- **"A principal can hand a teacher the whole thing on a Friday"** — that is the sentence a principal will remember.
- **Coffee.** You are both in Maryland.
