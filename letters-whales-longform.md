# Whales — the long-form rewrite

The 43 whales that never got a proper letter. The top 25 file covers the other 19.

**What changed.** These were 550–800 characters of clean argument. Clean argument is what
everybody sends. These now run 1,200–1,900 — still a fraction of the ~8,000 a 1st-degree
LinkedIn message allows — and every one of them carries **one concrete image**, taken from
`reference/phrasebank.md`, because 97% of the short letters had none.

**Rules held throughout.**
- One gem per letter. No gem appears more than twice in the file.
- The read-along stays in all of them: **Lexile AD 620L, Adult Directed** — a cataloging
  fact, not a claim.
- Deadpan for program officers and federal staff. The claw machine does not go to a
  regulator.
- No Aisle Five reveal. No last-name wordplay.
- Dollar figures exact: **$25** and **$9.99** for the claw machine, **$293** shoes,
  **$69.50** shirt, both paid at **one cent**. $19.99 for the book. 25 copies = $499.75.
- The shirt has **not been worn in public.** "Had printed," never "have been wearing."
- Every letter ends on a question the recipient can actually answer.

**Two audit flags that are intentional — do not "fix" them.**
1. **Morgenson (1) does not use the stock spending-first phrasing.** She gets it in her own
   terms instead: *"We teach them to hold money. Nobody teaches them what happens when they
   use it."* That is better than the formula. Leave it.
2. **Concrete images sit at roughly half the file, by design.** Larrimore, Shrimali, Coogan,
   Pacchetti, Levine and the other program officers get deadpan. A claw machine in a letter
   to a federal researcher reads as unserious and costs the ask.
