## The Delaware setup — why this state is different

Three facts do all the work in these letters. Get them right.

1. **Delaware's State Board of Education adopted financial literacy standards for grades
   K–12 in 2018** (14 DE Admin. Code 501). K–5 has had standards for eight years.
2. **HB 203 was signed by Gov. Matt Meyer on October 9, 2025.** It requires a half-credit
   personal finance course to graduate, starting with students entering 9th grade in
   2026–27. Delaware became the 30th state to require it. All of the money, training, and
   press went to high school.
3. **House Resolution 31 ordered a K–12 curriculum alignment study**, run by the Delaware
   DOE with UD's Center for Economic Education & Entrepreneurship and UD's Institute for
   Public Administration. Report dated May 1, 2025. Districts were told to file their
   Financial Literacy Instructional Plans — by grade cluster, starting at K–3 — to
   `finlit.doe@doe.k12.de.us`.

So: elementary principals in Delaware carry a standards obligation that predates the new
law by seven years, got none of the HB 203 attention, and have a state mailbox already
collecting their K–3 plans. That is the whole pitch, and it is true.

Why she is the single best target in this entire outreach effort, Delaware or otherwise:

- She **directed development of the Voluntary National Content Standards in Economics** and
  **co-authored the National Standards for Financial Literacy**. The CEE framework on the
  standards grid at `clarencegetsabargain.com` is partly her work.
- She built **Economics for Kids**, a K and 1st-grade curriculum in which each lesson is a
  binder built around a **children's picture book** — *Monster Musical Chairs* for scarcity,
  *The Little Red Hen Makes Pizza* for producers and consumers, *Bunny Money* for savings
  goals. Colonial School District adopted it as its official kindergarten economics
  curriculum. Her method and this book are the same idea.
- On waiting for high school, she is on the record: **"It's too late if we wait until
  they're high school students."** Reproduce verbatim or not at all.
- Former **President, National Association of Economic Educators**. Delaware Women's Hall of
  Fame. Board member, Delaware Financial Literacy Institute. Roughly 50 years in.
- Ph.D. and M.Ed. from Delaware; B.A. Ohio Wesleyan, 1967.

If one book gets mailed in Delaware, it goes to her. She reviews children's literature for
economics content as an actual job function.
