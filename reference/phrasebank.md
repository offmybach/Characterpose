## 🥇 The best thing in the entire history, and it is autobiography

From `linkedin-posts-educator-toolkit.md` @7b1be98, "Week 2 — First Memory (Yours)."
In no letter, on no page, in no live file.

> "I was at a grocery store with my dad. He handed me a $20 and told me to fill the
> cart for $19. He'd carry the change home. I remember staring at a bag of grapes
> that was $3.49 and trying to calculate whether I could afford a second snack if I
> committed to the grapes.

>

> putting actual items on the conveyor belt while my dad watched.
>

> I was technically ready."

**Why this outranks every constructed line in the project.** The whole thesis is
*spending comes before saving*. This is the author, at seven, being handed money to
**spend** — and losing. It is specific ($20, $19, $3.49), self-deprecating (he lost,
and it was Doritos), and it is the only asset that explains *why he wrote this book
instead of another saving book* without asserting anything.

⚠️ **Verify before it ships.** If this is a real memory, it is the strongest cold-open
available for press, podcasts, parenting orgs, and the CFPB letter. If any detail is
approximate, fix the detail — but do not smooth the story. The Doritos are the point.

**$25 into the claw machine. The prize is a basketball. Walmart sells that basketball
for $9.99.**

Confirmed by Jonathan 30 Jul 2026. A recalled version — a dollar a try, sixteen tries,
a $10 toy — was **a guess and is retired.** Do not use it, and do not blend the two.

percentage — the raw pair does the work and the written post is right to skip the math.

## The written version, verbatim

> **The $25 Basketball**
>
> The claw machine ate $25 tonight. The prize: a basketball.
> Walmart sells that basketball for $9.99.
>
> **That's the entire boardwalk business model in one glass box — charge $25 for the $9.99
> thing and make it feel like winning.**
>
> Kids who've read *Clarence Gets a Bargain* run this math on their own… **Once a kid has
> seen the $9.99, the claw loses its grip.**
>
> One book. Every glass box on the boardwalk, defused.

Engagement hook underneath it, the best comment-bait in the bank:
> "Most money you've ever watched someone feed a claw machine? Dollar figures only. **This is
> a safe space.**"

## Why it earns a place in cold letters

It is the book's thesis inverted. Clarence compares two robots on a shelf and takes the
cheaper one on purpose. Sixty feet up the boardwalk another six-year-old hands over $25 for
a $9.99 basketball and calls it winning. Only one of them was taught.

It also gives the boardwalk shirt its punchline. The shirt says PREVENT BOARDWALK MELTDOWNS
& ARCADE HISSY-FITS, ASK ME HOW. Until now there was no answer to "how."
