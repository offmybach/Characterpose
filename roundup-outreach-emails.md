## Before you hit send — the 30-second checklist

1. Open their list. Make sure the book you're complimenting is still on it. Forbes blocked a
   direct read on 24 Sep, so *Little Critter* in email #1 is confirmed from search results
   only. Check that one first.
2. Swap every `[bracket]`. If a bracket ships, you've lost.
3. Offer the hardback, never a PDF. A book on a desk gets read; a PDF gets a "thanks!"
4. One ask per email: add it, or take a copy. Nothing else.
5. Don't send the spoiler. The P.S. lines tease the aisle sign. None of them say what it reads.

## Not on the list, and why

- **U.S. News, "Best Money Books for Kids"** — the page wouldn't load on 24 Sep and the byline
  isn't findable from search. Open it, get the writer's name, and adapt email #3. Staff
  journalists don't mail-order books, so skip the hardback offer and link Book Facts instead.
- **Brightly (readbrightly.com)** — owned by Penguin Random House. Its lists mostly feature its
  own house's books. Low odds.
- **BooksForTopics** — a UK site pitched at ages 3–5. Dollars, sales tax, and a 529 don't
  travel.
- **Money Parents** — no byline, prices in pounds, teen-heavy list.
- **Programming Librarian "7 Picture Books"** — 2022 and not being updated. It's the same ALA
  office as #6, so that email covers it.
