FROM THE INTRODUCTION

A Statement for the Record

One summer of beach code enforcement. Sworn, notarized, and still annoyed.

I wrote this book out of frustration. Not the ordinary kind — the kind you earn by spending a summer in code enforcement with a beach patrol. The offenses weren’t just constant. Some were so inventive there was no precedent for handling them. And enforcement had teeth of Silly Putty. Compliance was theater — it lasted exactly as long as I was in view, and the beach is long.

By the third pass my opening line had worn down to “Did I stutter the first two times?” — which appears nowhere in the training manual. On the rare occasion a citation actually materialized, it ran twenty-five dollars. On a beach holding ten thousand people, twenty-five dollars isn’t deterring anybody. It’s a cover charge, and everybody knows it.

Full disclosure: I am not, strictly speaking, a rule follower. I’m a rule reader — completely different animal. I read every posted sign, voluntarily, and then I sort them. The rules protecting the person on the next blanket get followed to the letter. The stupid ones get bent as far as the material allows. My violations come with a legal theory. Yours come with a Bluetooth speaker.

The rules that follow are common sense — for those who possess any. For everyone else, I wrote them down.

THE DOCKET

Seventeen Rules. All Posted. All Ignored.

Each one earned the hard way, at close range, downwind. A sampling of the charges:

RULE 01

Personal Space

🦀Art en routecaption: all this: available

Four thousand feet of beach. He chose these four.

Prime oceanfront, zero money down, no closing costs — and you passed on the deal of the century to set up camp on top of me.

RULE 02

The Smoke Show

🦀Art en routecaption: *cough*

Local forecast: dense fog, 100% chance of you. Aircraft diverting.

Your vape cloud has its own weather pattern, and it smells like a combination of skunk and cotton candy.

RULE 03

Audio Terrorism

🦀Art en routecaption: (bass, via skeleton)

The set list nobody requested, at a volume nobody survived.

Nobody within earshot arranged your playlist. We are hostages. And we are not enjoying the set.

RULE 04

The Excavators

🦀Art en routecaption: depth: one toddler, and holding

It’s not a hole. It’s a lawsuit with a view.

That hole you dug, with the diameter of my kitchen table and the depth of a World War I trench, is an open grave. Fill it.

RULE 05

Fire & Flight Hazards

🦀Art en routecaption: incoming!

An $85 nylon death bird, cleared for one more victim.

Leave the fireworks to the professionals; they spend 364 days a year training for this one day. You bought yours from a tent in the parking lot off the highway.

RULE 06

Shanty Towns & Projectiles

🦀Art en routecaption: four stakes. he used none.

The cabana has its own ZIP code.

An umbrella that isn’t buried to the hilt is a javelin on standby. I’ve been hit by one, and the pointed end missed my face by inches.

RULE 07

Trash & Beasts

🦀Art en routecaption: witness / the muscle / the don / the lookout

A crime family with wings. They know you have fries.

Throw one fry onto the sand and you’ve rung the dinner bell for the entire flock to mug the rest of us. You just became their supplier.

RULE 08

Unsupervised Chaos

🦀Art en routecaption: nice spiral / the nachos never saw it coming

The game concluded on impact. Ball impounded. Rules are rules.

A jam-packed beach is not a sporting venue. Your perfect spiral has a flight path, and it terminates in someone’s nachos.

RULE 09

The Exfoliators

🦀Art en routecaption: wind / pizza, now SPF 50

Basting everyone downwind. Coconut and zinc oxide, family style.

Snapping your towel into open air is the “I’m outta here, so you all get exfoliated” maneuver, and it is a coward’s farewell.

RULE 10

The Nuisance Trifecta

🦀Art en routecaption: Big Brother, hovering / one sibling too many

Big Brother with a camera. I have enough siblings.

There is a fine line between “vacation mode” and “public nuisance,” and you, sir, are drinking Jack out of the bottle at 10:47 a.m. on a Tuesday. That line is a mile behind you.

RULE 11

Authority

🦀Art en routecaption: one of them is watching the water

They’re scanning the water. You’re scanning them.

The lifeguard isn’t judging your buzz. He’s mentally calculating how much you’ll weigh soaking wet.

RULE 12

The Ocean Is Not a Toilet

🦀Art en routecaption: nope. / nope nope. / suspiciously relaxed

The “Eh, it’s all water” doctrine, rejected by every court and both swimmers.

Shared water is not a private commode. The fact that I must put this in writing, with a straight face, is its own small tragedy.

RULE 13

The Squatters

🦀Art en routecaption: 6:00 a.m. / population: zero / my chair, two feet closer

Ten empty chairs. One occupied one, two feet in front of them.

You cannot hold the front row with a flip-flop and good intentions. First-come, first-seated. So come.

RULE 14

Rods & Boards

🦀Art en routecaption: not a striper / the flags / take it past the flags

Catch and release. The hook still hurts.

Your treble hook does not distinguish between a striper and a second-grader. Take it past the flags, where the fish are plentiful and the second-graders are not.

RULE 15

Personal Maintenance

🦀Art en routecaption: ten small crescents of you, airborne

Grooming with a blast radius. The umbrella was a lifestyle choice.

Clipping your toenails on the beach launches ten small crescents of you into the general population. All of it belongs behind a locked door, ideally in another state.

RULE 16

Parenting

🦀Art en routecaption: “with YOU!” / “with YOU!” / exhibit A, walking

“I thought he was with you.” Nobody was with him.

The ocean does not babysit. It has no references, no certifications, and a record. An adult goes in too, or no one does.

RULE 17

The Golden Rule

🦀Art en routecaption: BEACH RULES / cannot read, will do the stupid thing anyway

They’re not decorative. Someone did the stupid thing before you.

Just read the signs. If reading requires too much effort, the universal symbols — the red circle, the slash — are not difficult to decipher.

That’s the sampler. The full charges — with evidence — are in the book.

QUESTIONS FROM THE PUBLIC

Asked, Answered, Filed.

Is this book about me?
Statistically? Yes. Page through it at the store. If your ears get hot anywhere between Rule 1 and Rule 17, that’s your receipt. Buy it.
Does the Crabby Guy have any actual authority?
He had a polo shirt, a whistle, and a summer of code enforcement behind him. Enforcement had teeth of Silly Putty; compliance was theater. The book is what’s left of the authority — and it costs the same as the citation nobody feared. $12.99 versus a $25 fine. The book is the better deal, and unlike the fine, people actually read it.
I follow the rules. Why would I need this?
You’re not buying it for you. Buy two: one for the beach bag, one to leave on the offender’s towel like a citation. Watching them reach page 11 costs nothing extra.
Is it for kids?
No. The kids already have a book with a robot in it. This one is about adults behaving badly, written for the adults stuck six feet downwind of them. The shame is calibrated for someone with a mortgage.
