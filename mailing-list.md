# Who gets a physical book

A picture book is a physical object. Hardbound, full color, 11 x 8.5. A flipbook link
cannot carry that, and the people below all judge books partly by holding them.

Cost is roughly **$25 a copy** landed, book plus media mail. The whole list below is
about $400. That is not the constraint. Postage discipline is.

---

## The rule: mail on a yes, not on a hunch

Send the message first. It is free, it is fast, and it ends with a small question.
When they answer — even "sure, send it" — the book arrives **expected**, addressed to
someone who already engaged. That book gets opened.

A cold parcel to a school or association office gets opened by an assistant, put on a
pile, and read by nobody.

**One exception:** if a top-tier target does not reply within about two weeks, mail it
anyway with a shorter note. At that point the object is the only argument left, and it
is a better argument than a fourth follow-up.

## Do not mail — digital is better

Podcasters, reporters, columnists, and academics. They want a hook, a fact, or a PDF
they can search. A parcel is friction for them, not flattery. The exception is a
reviewer who asks for a physical copy, which some will.

**The Stanford three fall here** — Annamaria Lusardi 🔵, Andrea Sticha 🐋, and Neale
Mahoney 🐋. All whales, all digital. Send the flipbook link and the argument, never a
box. Letters and the full reasoning are in `jumpstart-clearinghouse-strategy.md`.

## The card that goes in the box

Handwrite it. Four lines maximum. The letter already did the arguing; this is only a
label so the parcel makes sense on arrival.

```
Dr. Edoho-Eket —

You said it started with five-year-olds in Howard County.
This is for them. Thirty-six pages, no worksheet, no piggy bank.

Tell me if it would survive a real classroom.

— Jonathan Bach, Baltimore
```

Do not reprint the pitch inside the parcel. They have it. Repeating it reads as
nerves.
