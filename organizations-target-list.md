# Organizations, Lists, and Channels — the non-obvious ones

Not people. Rooms, lists, and doors.

Everything below was checked in July 2026. Where an eligibility rule would disqualify a
self-published title, I say so and put it on the cut list instead of burying it. Where I
could not confirm a rule, I say that too — do not assume the answer, call and ask.

The through-line: you are competing for a slot that already exists. Somebody's book is
already on the Fed's elementary list. Somebody's book is already in the credit union's
schools program. There is no need to create demand; there is a need to take a seat.

# LANE 1 — School librarians

They buy, they recommend, they never get pitched by financial literacy people. This is the
softest lane on the page.

### AASL — American Association of School Librarians (ALA division)
`aasl@ala.org` · 225 N. Michigan Ave., Suite 1300, Chicago, IL 60601 · 800-545-2433

AASL runs a **Money Smart Week** page for school librarians. A national librarian
association that already publishes financial-literacy programming guidance, and the books
they point to are all savings books. Annual national conference.

### Money Smart Week — ALA + Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago 🔑
`moneysmartweek.org` · every **April**

A national initiative between ALA and the Chicago Fed. Libraries of every type register
free programming under the banner — the topic list already includes **"saving money
through couponing."** That is your book, verbatim, sitting in their own copy.

**This is the cheapest national visibility on this entire page.** You register an event.
It costs nothing. You can do it at Rehoboth Beach Public Library, at Lewes, in Baltimore,
or all three, and every one of them appears on a national map in April.

### ALA's financial literacy collection guide
ALA maintains a FINRA-funded LibGuide, *Financial Literacy in Public Libraries: A Guide for
Building Collections*, plus a Literacy Clearinghouse financial-literacy page. Getting a
title listed in a collection-development guide is worth more than a review, because
librarians read it while holding a purchase order.

### MASL — Maryland Association of School Librarians
111 Tail of the Fox Dr., Ocean Pines, MD 21811 · 410-929-1181 · `maslmd.org`
Annual conference. Home state. Ask about an author table or a session.

### DASL — Delaware Association of School Librarians
President **Stephanie Saggione**; Vice President **Peggy Griffith**. Sits under the Delaware
Library Association, c/o Delaware Division of Libraries, 121 Martin Luther King Jr. Blvd.
N., Dover, DE 19901. Small state, small association, easy to reach a president.

### ALSC — Association for Library Service to Children (ALA)
Notable Children's Books list. Public-library side rather than school, but the same buyers.

# LANE 2 — Lists and awards school buyers actually read

### ⏰ Bank Street College — Best Children's Books of the Year — DEADLINE NOV 1, 2026
`bookcom@bankstreet.edu`
The Children's Book Committee, Bank Street College of Education, 610 West 112th Street,
New York, NY 10025

**One copy. One stamp. Deadline is November 1 of the year the book was published**, which
for you is roughly three months from now. This is the single most time-sensitive item on
this page.

Bank Street is the credential that librarians and educators recognize without being told
what it is. Their published guidelines say nothing excluding self-published or
independently published titles — they ask only that "publisher contact information must be
included with each shipment," and you are the publisher. If you want certainty before
mailing, email `bookcom@bankstreet.edu` and ask in one line. Then mail it either way.

### NCSS / CBC — Notable Social Studies Trade Books for Young People
**Laura Peraza**, `laura.peraza@cbcbooks.org` · general: `cbc.info@cbcbooks.org` ·
212-966-1990
Submissions for the next cycle **open spring 2027.**

The best-fit list in existence for this book. Run jointly by the National Council for the
Social Studies and the Children's Book Council since 1972, K–12, selected by social studies
educators — and economics sits inside the social studies strand. Their stated criteria
include "an original theme or a fresh slant on a traditional topic," which is the argument
you have been making in every letter for six months.

**Unresolved:** whether the submitting publisher must be a CBC member. Their page doesn't
say and I would not guess. Email Peraza and ask before spring 2027; the answer changes
whether this costs a stamp or a membership.

### EIFLE Awards — Institute for Financial Literacy
`EIFLE@FinancialLit.org` · `eifleawards.org` · **$75 entry** · deadline mid-**January**
(2026 cycle closed January 16)

Book of the Year categories, including children's. Twenty years running in the financial
literacy world specifically, which is the world you are selling into.

**You have a warm hand here and should use it.** Wally Luckeydoo was named 2026 EIFLE
Educator of the Year and took the Jump$tart Corey Carlisle Award the same year. He already
endorsed the book. Ask him whether a nomination from a recent EIFLE honoree carries weight
— worst case he says it doesn't.

### The pay-to-enter indie awards
Foreword INDIES, IBPA Benjamin Franklin, Moonbeam Children's Book Awards, Purple Dragonfly,
Mom's Choice, Next Generation Indie. All accept self-published titles. All charge. Their
credibility runs from real (Foreword, IBPA) to sticker-on-a-cover (the rest).

Honest read: a Foreword INDIES or Benjamin Franklin finalist line is worth having on the
press kit. The others buy you a foil sticker and a press release nobody reads. Pick one or
two, not six.

# LANE 3 — Economic education, where a picture book is already the delivery vehicle

This lane matters more than the awards lane, because these people **build lessons around
children's books as their day job.** They are not being asked to like your book. They are
being asked to add one to a collection they already run.

### 🔑 EconEdLink — "Economics and Personal Finance in Children's Literature"
Council for Economic Education · `econedlink.org/contact/`

A standing collection of **26+ lessons built around picture books** for grades K–2, 3–5, and
6–8. Current titles include *Alexander, Who Used to Be Rich Last Sunday*, *A Chair for My
Mother*, *Arthur's Pet Business*, *One Hen*.

Read that list again. Every one of them is about earning, saving, or losing money. Not one
of them walks a child through a purchase. There is a hole in a collection that CEE
maintains, and CEE is one of the five frameworks already on your standards grid.

Their page publishes no submission process. That is an opportunity, not an obstacle — write
to the contact form and propose the lesson, not the book. Offer to write it yourself.

### 🔑 St. Louis Fed — Econ Lowdown
`stlouisfed.org/education`

**Over 50 elementary lessons using children's literature**, plus a set of Parent Q&A
resources built around specific titles (*Alexander, Who Used to Be Rich Last Sunday*; *Just
Saving My Money*; *Little Nino's Pizzeria*). Their econ ed materials have won National
Association of Economic Educators awards.

Same pitch as EconEdLink, different building. These two are the highest-value targets in
the whole document after Bank Street's deadline.

### Federal Reserve Bank of Philadelphia — economic education
Delaware and eastern Pennsylvania sit in the Third District, and this team is already
building Delaware's HB 203 materials with UD.

- **Andrew Hill** — Assistant Vice President
- **Todd Zartman** — Economic Education Manager
- **Rebecca Chambers** — Economic Education Adviser
- **Meaghan Vitale** — Economic Education Specialist

### Maryland Council on Economic Education — Towson University
8000 York Road, Towson, MD 21252 · **410-704-2137** · `econed.org`

Your home-state CEE affiliate, twenty minutes from your desk. Sources disagree on who holds
which title — I found both Julie Weaver and Dawn Baker attached to leadership, and their
staff page 404s. **Call and ask for the elementary programs person by role, not by name.**

### National Association of Economic Educators
Bonnie Meszaros is a past president. If she engages, this is the next door and she is the
one who opens it.

# LANE 4 — When the buyer isn't the school 🔑

The best lane on this page, and the least obvious. In this lane nobody asks a principal for
a budget line, because a bank pays for it.

### The Berenstain Bears proof
The **National Credit Union Foundation** actively encourages credit unions to run **The
Berenstain Bears Financial Literacy Program** in elementary schools. The **Credit Union
Network for Financial Literacy (CUNFL)** is its exclusive purveyor. The package is a
children's book series plus lesson plans plus a savings account for the kid, and the credit
union's name goes on it.

So the model is proven, funded, and currently occupied by bears.

**The pitch is not "buy my book."** It is: sponsor a classroom set, put your logo on a
bookplate inside the front cover, and get a Community Reinvestment Act activity out of it.
You already built the CRA memo — it is sitting in `resources/grant-in-a-box.html`.

Targets: National Credit Union Foundation · America's Credit Unions · MD|DC Credit Union
Association · individual credit unions in Baltimore and Sussex County.

### ABA Foundation — Teach Children to Save
Every April. Bank employees walk into elementary classrooms and need something to hand out.
You have already read their 25-title book list end to end, which means you know exactly
what is on it and exactly what isn't. That list is the target.

### Delaware Financial Education Alliance (Delaware Bankers Association)
Runs Teach Children to Save Day, **Wizzo the Money Dog**, **The Great Investo**, and **More
Money Than Cents** — character-driven elementary programs. A picture book fits a shelf they
already stock. Details in `contacts-delaware.md`.

# LANE 5 — The author-in-schools market (this one pays you)

Every other lane sells books. This one books work.

### 🔑 Arts for Learning Maryland (formerly Young Audiences of Maryland)
`artsforlearningmd.org` · K–12 Teaching Artist Roster

They place teaching artists into Maryland schools for residencies and assemblies, and they
already run **Author/Illustrator programs** — including one artist who presents on how
children's books get made, covering writing, illustration, and publishing.

That is your program, except yours has a second act. You wrote it, you drew it, and you are
a real estate finance attorney who can explain a markdown to a nine-year-old. Nobody else on
that roster has that combination.

Apply to the K–12 roster. Then the residency work is paid, recurring, and puts the book in
a building with you standing next to it.

### Maryland State Arts Council — Teaching Artist Roster
`msac.org/programs/arts-education/teaching-artist-roster`. State-level version of the same
thing, and MSAC roster status is a credential districts recognize.

### Delaware Division of the Arts — artist roster / Arts in Education
The Delaware equivalent. Check residency requirements before applying.

### Junior Achievement 🔑
**JA of Central Maryland** — `centralmaryland.ja.org` · **JA of Delaware** —
`delaware.ja.org`

**JA Ourselves**, their kindergarten program, "uses storybook characters in read-aloud and
hands-on activities to introduce the role people play in an economy." A read-aloud program.
That is the whole delivery mechanism.

Both of your geographies have a JA office. Volunteer first, present the book second — a
volunteer who has actually stood in the classroom gets a different hearing than an author
with an email.

### Nerdy Book Club / nErDcamp
`nerdybookclub.wordpress.com`

A grassroots community of teachers, librarians, and authors — nErDcamp drew 1,400
educators plus 1,000 kids at nErDcamp Junior. The blog takes guest posts; there is a sign-up
form on the site.

Free, and it reaches the exact people you have been emailing one at a time. Write the "I
read every children's money book and they all teach saving" post. That is a Nerdy Book Club
post, not an ad.

# LANE 6 — Distribution channels nobody thinks about

### 🔑 EBMA — Educational Book & Media Association
`admin@edupaperback.org` · **540-318-7770** · P.O. Box 335, Heber City, UT 84032
Annual meeting: **January**

Founded 1975. Two membership classes: wholesale distributors in the educational book
business, and **associate members — "publishers and national distributors committed to the
educational book business."** You would join as the second one.

Here is why this is on the page. At the annual meeting they run **One-on-One Sessions:
every wholesaler in attendance meets every publisher member for a private eight-minute
discussion.**

These are the companies that stock school and library orders. One January meeting is a
speed-dating round with the people who actually put books into school libraries — a room a
self-published author has essentially no other way to enter.

Dues are not published. Call and ask what associate membership costs and whether a
single-title publisher qualifies. If the number is under a few thousand dollars, this is the
best-value item in this entire document.

### First Book / First Book Marketplace
Distributes to Title I schools and programs at scale. The educator-side signup is public;
the publisher-side process is not documented anywhere I could find. Ask them directly.

### NAIBA — New Atlantic Independent Booksellers Association
Territory: **NY, PA, NJ, DE, MD, WV, DC, and northern Virginia** — which is your entire
map. Authors whose work sells through indie bookstores may join; the board approves
applications. Annual fall conference with author programming.

### Browseabout Books — Rehoboth Beach
Independent since 1975, and they feature local authors and their books. You live there part
of the year. Walk in.

### 🔑 Beachside Book Festival — Rehoboth Beach
Sussex County Libraries in partnership with Browseabout Books · Rehoboth Beach Convention
Center, 229 Rehoboth Ave. · **April**

Local author talks, books for sale, Cape Region vendors. David Baldacci headlined the 2025
edition, so the traffic is real. Application details for the next one were not posted when I
checked — ask Sussex County Libraries in the winter.

### Biblion — Lewes
The other Cape Region indie.

# LANE 7 — Youth organizations that already have a money badge

### 🔑 Girl Scouts
Financial Literacy badges **and** Cookie Business badges at every level from Brownie up, and
GSUSA rebuilt the financial literacy curriculum in partnership with **Charles Schwab**.

Councils to approach: **Girl Scouts of Central Maryland** and **Girl Scouts of the
Chesapeake Bay** (Delaware plus the Eastern Shore). Two councils, both yours.

The angle is a badge workshop, not a book sale. A troop earns a badge; the book is the
curriculum; the council shop stocks it. Cookie season is the whole conceit of your book
running in real life — a Brownie setting a sales goal is doing comparison math whether
anybody names it or not.

### 4-H
National 4-H has financial literacy curriculum, and Delaware 4-H runs through UD Cooperative
Extension — the same university as CEEE. One introduction covers both.

### National PTA / Maryland PTA / Delaware PTA
PTAs run grants and family nights and have their own money. A PTA-funded classroom set
never touches a school budget, which removes the objection you hear most.
