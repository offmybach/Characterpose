## These are all British, and that changes the pitch

Every one of the five writes for a UK audience: This is Money / Daily Mail, the Financial Times,
Incisive Media, and two freelances. The book is aggressively American — Sea-Mart, a 529 account,
Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Prime Day — and one difference matters more than the rest:

**Sales tax is the book's climax, and Britain doesn't have it the same way.** On page 22 Clarence
learns at the till that the sticker price was never the price, because American sales tax is added
at checkout. In the UK, VAT is already inside the number on the shelf. So a British six-year-old
never gets that lesson, and an American one gets it the first time they buy anything.

Pretending that gap isn't there would waste the approach. **Leading with it is the better story** —
a money journalist can do something with "the central lesson of this book does not translate to
your country," and nothing at all with "please review my picture book." Four of the five letters
below make the transatlantic difference the pitch, and offer the book only as ballast.
