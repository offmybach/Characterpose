# DISCUSSION GUIDE
## Clarence Gets a Bargain
**Chapter-by-Chapter Facilitation Guide · Mapped to Jump$tart, CEE, and CCSS**

For educators, librarians, and community facilitators. Three depth levels per section:

- **[L] Literal** — direct from the text.
- **[I] Inferential** — between the lines.
- **[E] Evaluative** — personal connection, real-world application.

Vocabulary words appear bolded in context. Full glossary at the end.

---

## Section 1 — "I'm Going to Get the Newest, Best One!" (Pages 1–3)

### Summary
Clarence has earned a robot. He's been talking about it all week. Mom reminds him the reward still costs money — "money doesn't grow on trees or get cranked out of a 3D printer." She walks him through the household bills: mortgage, cars, clothes, Xbox, hot water, fridge, internet, YouTube. Things cost money to run.

### Vocabulary
- **Reward** — Something earned for doing something good.
- **Bills** — Money a family pays every month for things they use.
- **Mortgage** — The special bill for a house.
- **Budget** — A plan for how to spend your money.
- **Need** — Something you can't go without.
- **Want** — Something you'd like to have but don't need to survive.

### Discussion Questions
- **[L]** What did Clarence do to earn his robot?
- **[L]** Name three things Mom says cost money to keep running.
- **[I]** Why does Mom bring up bills when Clarence is excited about the robot?
- **[I]** What's the difference between "I want a robot" and "I need food"?
- **[E]** What's something at YOUR house that costs money you didn't know about?
- **[E]** Could YOU live without YouTube? For how long?

**Standards:** Jump$tart SPD-1; CEE Standard 2.1; FDIC Money Smart.

## Section 2 — "Shopping Homework" (Pages 4–10)

### Summary
Mom asks Clarence to bring a pile of newspapers from the kitchen. She opens the ad inserts and tells him he has homework before the trip: figure out where the best deal is. She introduces sales (items sometimes cost less than usual) and comparison shopping (checking deals across multiple stores' ads). She mentions setting money aside for charity. She introduces the 529 college account. Then coupons get a section of their own — cut out from ads, sent by mail or email, even texted to phones. Together they find the best robot deal at Sea-Mart.

### Vocabulary
- **Sale** — When something costs less than its usual price.
- **Sale ad** — A flyer or newspaper insert that lists sale prices.
- **Comparison shopping** — Checking prices at more than one place before buying.
- **Coupon** — A piece of paper, email, or code that lowers the price.
- **Charity** — Giving money or things to people who need help.
- **529 account** — A special savings account for college.

### Discussion Questions
- **[L]** What did Mom call "homework that's not so bad"?
- **[L]** Name three ways a coupon can reach you (paper, mail, email, text).
- **[I]** Why is comparing ads BEFORE going to the store smarter than walking in cold?
- **[I]** Why does Mom bring up charity in the middle of a shopping lesson?
- **[E]** Have you ever seen a coupon at home? What was it for?
- **[E]** If you were saving money in a 529 for college, what would you want to study?

**Standards:** Jump$tart SPD-2, SPD-3, SVG-1; CEE Standard 2; CCSS Math 2.MD.8.

## Section 3 — "Aren't You a Smart Little Shopper?" (Pages 11–18)

### Summary
Clarence walks into Sea-Mart already knowing where to ask. The lady in the red vest points him to the toy robots and calls him a smart little shopper. He sees a huge selection — short ones, tall ones, red ones, black ones. Then he rounds the corner and sees a giant neon-orange sign with his name on it. (Reader, it does not say "Clarence." It says "Clearance.") Mom uses the moment to teach the clearance section: items get moved there when the store needs the space, often because a newer model just came out. Managers "mark down" the regular price.

### Vocabulary
- **Clearance** — A discount section where items get reduced prices.
- **Markdown** — Lowering the regular price.
- **Newer model** — A more recent version of a product.

### Discussion Questions
- **[L]** Why does the manager move items to the clearance section?
- **[L]** What color sticker does Mom say to look for?
- **[I]** Why might a "newer" version of something cost more than an older one?
- **[I]** Does "older" mean "broken"? How do you know?
- **[E]** Have you ever found a clearance sticker on something at a store? What was it?
- **[E]** Would you be willing to buy something with an older version if it worked just as well?

**Standards:** Jump$tart SPD-3; CEE Standard 2.3; CCSS ELA RI.3.1.

## Section 4 — "Only Two Differences" (Pages 19–22)

### Summary
Clarence finds a RoBimmie on the clearance shelf — the one he had in mind. He takes it to the aisle of newer robots and compares them side by side. Two differences: one has a slightly bigger screen, one has an antenna. He picks the clearance robot. At checkout, Mom mentions sales tax (the total at the register will be higher than the tag). Then Clarence remembers — there's a 10%-off-clearance-toys coupon in his pocket from the morning's ad. He tears it off and hands it to the cashier. Decision becomes an even greater value.

### Vocabulary
- **Sales tax** — Extra money the store collects for the state on most purchases.
- **Cashier** — The person who takes your payment.
- **Value** — How much something is worth for what it costs.

### Discussion Questions
- **[L]** What were the only two differences between the clearance robot and the new ones?
- **[L]** What does Mom warn Clarence about right before they get to the checkout?
- **[I]** Why is the clearance robot still a smart pick if it's "older"?
- **[I]** How did the coupon make the deal even better?
- **[E]** Would you have picked the clearance robot or the new one? Why?
- **[E]** Have you ever been surprised by the total at a register? (Sales tax!)

**Standards:** Jump$tart SPD-3, FM-1; CEE Standard 2; CCSS Math 4.NF.6, 5.NBT.7.

## Section 5 — "We May Have Exaggerated" (Pages 23–26)

### Summary
At home, Dad is impressed. So impressed that he confesses: Mom and Dad were kind of exaggerating about the cold baths and Wi-Fi. The bills get paid fine. They just wanted Clarence to understand that savings matter. The 529 is still going. Clarence has a follow-up realization on page 25: a good deal isn't a deal if you don't use the thing. The smart robot has math and word games. He'll use it. From here on out, Clarence is the kid hunting deals on everything, not just toys.

### Vocabulary
- **Exaggerated** — Made something sound bigger or worse than it really is.
- **Savings** — Money set aside to use later.
- **Smart shopper** — Someone who thinks before they buy.

### Discussion Questions
- **[L]** What did Dad admit Mom and Dad were exaggerating about?
- **[L]** Why does Clarence think the smart robot was a good buy?
- **[I]** Why would parents pretend they couldn't pay bills?
- **[I]** What's the difference between "a good deal" and "something you actually need"?
- **[E]** Have you ever bought (or asked for) something just because it was on sale and then never used it?
- **[E]** If your family had to cut one bill for a month, which one would you pick?

**Standards:** Jump$tart SVG-1, SVG-2, FM-1; CEE Standard 5; FDIC Money Smart.

---

## Section 6 — "Just Wait Till I Teach You About Black Friday." (Pages 27–32)

### Summary
Clarence brings the lessons home. He and Mom make games out of finding deals at the grocery store — loser carries the bags. The family invents "Guess the Price" — after dinner, whoever got a great deal that day quizzes everyone to guess closest. Clarence figures that out fast: bid low. He learns that sometimes the best lessons happen with parents, not at school. The book closes with Mom hinting at the sequel — online shopping, Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Prime Day.

### Vocabulary
- **Habit** — Something you do regularly.
- **Black Friday / Cyber Monday / Prime Day** — Big sale days that happen once a year.

### Discussion Questions
- **[L]** What two games does Clarence's family invent?
- **[L]** What does Mom say she'll teach Clarence next?
- **[I]** Why does Clarence's family make a GAME out of saving money?
- **[I]** Why might "parents can teach you cool stuff that you don't learn in school" be true?
- **[E]** What money game could YOU play with your family this week?
- **[E]** Have you ever shopped on Black Friday or Cyber Monday with your family? What was it like?

**Standards:** Jump$tart SVG-2, FM-1; CEE Standard 5.2.
