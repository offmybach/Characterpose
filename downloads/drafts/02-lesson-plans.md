# 45-MINUTE CLASSROOM LESSON PLANS
## Clarence Gets a Bargain

**Four weekly sessions · Grades 1–5 · No prep required**

Each lesson uses Clarence Gets a Bargain as the anchor text and aligns to Jump$tart, CEE, and Common Core standards. Modules can stand alone or run as the full 4-week module.

---

## Week 1 — "Don't Forget, Today's the Day."
### Wants, Needs, and the Bills Speech

**Pages read:** 1–3
**Grades:** 1–5
**Standards:** Jump$tart SPD-1; CEE Standard 2; CCSS ELA SL.1–5, RI.1–5.1

### Learning Objectives
- Define wants and needs in their own words.
- Identify three real bills a household pays.
- Sort items into wants vs. needs.
- Explain why Clarence's robot is a want, not a need.

### Materials
- Clarence Gets a Bargain (1 copy or projected)
- Wants vs. Needs sort cards (printable)
- Whiteboard / chart paper
- Crayons or markers

### Lesson Steps

**Warm-up (5 min).** Show six pictures: a bowl of soup, an Xbox, a hot bath, a robot toy, a winter coat, a YouTube ad. Thumbs up for need, thumbs down for want. Argue out loud about anything fuzzy.

**Read aloud (12 min).** Read pages 1–3. Pause after Mom's bills speech (mortgage, Xbox power, hot bath, fridge, internet, YouTube). Ask: *"Did you know your house came with a bill called a mortgage?"* Let kids react.

**Discussion (8 min).**
- What does Clarence want? (a smart robot)
- Why isn't his robot a need?
- What's one need Mom mentioned that you didn't know about?
- What's the difference between "I want a robot" and "I need food"?

**Activity (15 min).** Pairs sort 10 cards into Needs / Wants. Early finishers add their own items. Discuss the trickier ones (winter coat? jacket? phone?) as a whole class.

**Closure (5 min).** Exit ticket: each kid draws one need and one want, labels them.

### Differentiation
- **1st–2nd:** picture-only sort cards.
- **3rd–5th:** add an "It Depends" pile and defend the placement.

## Week 2 — "Shopping Homework? Yes, Really."
### Ad Comparison, Coupons, and Picking a Store

**Pages read:** 4–10
**Grades:** 1–5
**Standards:** Jump$tart SPD-2, SPD-3; CEE Standard 2; CCSS Math 2.MD.8, 3.NBT; CCSS ELA RI.1–5.7

### Learning Objectives
- Define sale, coupon, and comparison shopping.
- Read a real sale ad and find a discounted item.
- Compare two stores' prices on the same item and pick the better deal.
- Name three ways stores deliver coupons (cut-out, mail, email, text).

### Materials
- Clarence Gets a Bargain
- Real (or printable) sale flyers from 3–4 different stores
- Comparison worksheet (included)
- Scissors
- Coupon cut-out template

### Lesson Steps

**Warm-up (5 min).** Hold up a Sunday newspaper or a stack of flyers. Ask: *"Has anyone seen these at home? Do you know what they're for?"* Many will say "recycling." Quote Clarence on page 10: he thought the same thing.

**Read aloud (12 min).** Read pages 4–10. Pause on page 4 (Mom asks Clarence to bring the newspapers) and ask: *"What's homework that doesn't feel like homework?"* Read through page 8 (coupons explained). Pause again: *"Where might you find a coupon today besides the newspaper?"* (mail, email, text codes).

**Guided practice (10 min).** Distribute a real ad flyer per pair. Pick one item — a backpack, juice box, robot toy. Pairs find the price in their flyer. Class compares: who has the cheapest? Same item, different stores, different prices — that's comparison shopping. Just like Clarence and Mom finding the best robot deal at Sea-Mart.

**Coupon activity (10 min).** Each pair cuts out a coupon (e.g., "25¢ off any toy" or "10% off"). Show three sale prices on the board. Pairs apply their coupon and write the new price. Which coupon saved the most?

**Closure (5 min).** Quick share-out: "Which store had the best deal in your group? How did you know?"

### Differentiation
- **1st–2nd:** stick to whole-dollar prices and "lower is better."
- **3rd–5th:** add percentage discounts and calculate exact savings.

## Week 3 — "Page 22. Aisle Five."
### Clearance, Markdowns, and the Two-Difference Decision

**Pages read:** 11–22
**Grades:** 1–5
**Standards:** Jump$tart SPD-3; CEE Standard 2; CCSS Math 2.MD.8, 4.NF.6; CCSS ELA RI.1–5.3

### Learning Objectives
- Define clearance and markdown.
- Explain why a clearance item isn't broken.
- Compare two similar items and justify the better-value pick.
- Apply a percentage-off coupon at "checkout."

### Materials
- Clarence Gets a Bargain
- Two near-identical classroom items at different prices (one "marked down")
- Receipts / price tags template
- Play money or printable coins
- Calculator (3rd–5th)

### Lesson Steps

**Warm-up (5 min).** Project an image of a giant neon-orange "CLEARANCE" sign. Ask: *"What does this word mean? Does it sound like anybody's name?"* Set up the joke before kids meet it on page 13.

**Read aloud (15 min).** Read pages 11–22 (the entire Sea-Mart sequence). Pause on page 15 when Clarence realizes the sign reads "Clearance," not "Clarence." Pause on page 17 when Mom explains markdowns ("a newer version is released… that's when a manager will lower or 'mark down' the regular price"). Pause on page 20 — *"Only two differences. A slightly bigger screen and an antenna. Would you pay extra for that?"*

**Concept anchor (5 min).** Write two definitions on the board, in kids' own words:
- **Clearance:** "The store needs the shelf space. Not broken. Just older."
- **Markdown:** "The new price is lower than the old price."

**Activity — The Two-Robot Test (15 min).** Set up two classroom items: one "new" at full price, one "marked down" with a clearance sticker. Pairs inspect both, write down the differences, and decide which to "buy" with play money. Then Clarence's twist: each pair gets a 10% off coupon. They apply it to their purchase and recalculate.

**Closure (5 min).** Pair-share: *"Did your group pick the clearance one? Why? What changed when the coupon came out?"*

### Differentiation
- **1st–2nd:** dollar amounts only; "the lower number is the better deal."
- **3rd–5th:** calculate 10% manually; introduce sales tax (page 22) — "the price at the register is higher than the price tag. Why?"

## Week 4 — "Clarence Did It. Your Kids Can Too."
### Family Money Games + Post-Assessment

**Pages read:** 23–32
**Grades:** 1–5
**Standards:** All Jump$tart, CEE, and CCSS standards from Weeks 1–3

### Learning Objectives
- Demonstrate mastery of the 6 core concepts.
- Show measurable improvement on the post-assessment.
- Design one family money game students could play at home.

### Materials
- Clarence Gets a Bargain
- Post-Assessment Worksheet
- Wyze Shopper Certificate (printable)
- Optional: grocery flyer for the in-class "Guess the Price" game

### Lesson Steps

**Read aloud (10 min).** Read pages 23–32. Pause on page 24 (Dad admits the exaggeration about cold baths and Wi-Fi). Ask: *"Why do you think Mom and Dad said that? Were they lying?"* Then page 25 — *"Just because it was a good deal doesn't mean you have to buy it."* Let kids react.

**Post-Assessment (12 min).** Students complete the post-assessment independently. Collect.

**Game — Guess the Price (13 min).** Show three real grocery items on the board (or hold them up). Each kid writes down their best guess at the sale price. Reveal one at a time — closest guess wins. Just like Clarence's family on page 28.

**Certificates (5 min).** Hand out Wyze Shopper Certificates. Each student writes one money habit they'll try at home (read an ad, ask about a coupon, spot a clearance sticker, etc.).

**Closure (5 min).** Whole-class reflection: *"What's one thing Clarence taught you about money that you'll remember next time you go shopping?"*

### Differentiation
- **1st–2nd:** simplified picture-based post-assessment.
- **3rd–5th:** add a short written response — *"Why did Clarence pick the clearance robot? Was it a smart choice? Explain."*
