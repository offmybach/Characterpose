## Post 1 — The Canteen Account

I ran out of canteen money halfway through week three of a four-week camp session.

Blow Pops, Fun Dip, a soda — every visit, because I thought that's what being cool looked like. Nobody was standing behind me at the counter. No adult to stop me, no rule to slow me down. I just spent the way six-year-olds spend when nobody's taught them the rate matters.

I spent the last week and a half of that summer watching everyone else buy candy.

Every kids' money book would've told me to save some of it. That was never the problem — the money was already mine and already spent. I just spent it at a pace nobody had ever shown me how to notice.

So I wrote the book I couldn't find. Clarence Gets a Bargain teaches a 6-year-old what to do the first time he's standing at a register holding money — before he learns to save it, because saving is the second thing a kid does with money. Spending is the first, and every other book in this category skips it.

16+ money concepts, none of them announced. 36 pages, ages 6–10.

## Post 2 — Keeper of Receipts

I've walked out of the same Nordstrom Rack twice paying one cent. Once for shoes that rang up compare-at $293. Once for a shirt at $69.50. I kept both tags — a penny off $293 is 99.997%, and I wanted proof for dinner.

Same instinct runs the book. I write about a kid learning to do this at six — not save money, spend it well, which is the harder skill and the one nobody teaches first. Clarence Gets a Bargain follows one purchase start to finish: the sale ad at home, the aisle, the markdown, the coupon at checkout, the sales tax nobody warned him about.

Attorney. Dad. Keeper of receipts.

$19.99, 36 pages, ages 6–10. clarencegetsabargain.com

## Post 3 — The Claw Machine

The claw machine ate $25 of a kid's night at the boardwalk. The prize: a basketball. Walmart sells the same basketball for $9.99.

That's the entire boardwalk business model in one glass box — charge $25 for the $9.99 thing and make it feel like winning.

Once a kid has seen the $9.99, the claw loses its grip. That's comparison shopping, and it's one of 16+ money concepts hiding inside Clarence Gets a Bargain — a picture book about a kid who reads the sale ads, compares two robots on a shelf, and hands the cashier a coupon like a tiny attorney presenting Exhibit A.

It doesn't read like a worksheet. It reads like a story a kid actually finishes, with a 21-term glossary in the back he won't notice he's already lived until he gets there.

$19.99. Ages 6–10. clarencegetsabargain.com
