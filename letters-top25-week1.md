## The thesis these are built on

Five things, in this order. Every letter uses at least three.

1. **Spending comes before saving.** Not a topic claim, a sequence claim. A six-year-old has
   no savings rate. She has stood at a register holding money.
2. **Idea to post-receipt.** The book follows one purchase the whole way — wanting the thing,
   earning it, the sale ads, the aisle, the markdown, the coupon, the sales tax, the receipt,
   and living with what you bought. Read every title on the ABA Foundation's list; not one
   does the full arc.
3. **First purchase makes them a consumer.** The moment money changes hands a child is a
   party to a transaction, with rights nobody tells them about.
4. **Smuggled, not taught.** Sixteen-plus concepts, none announced. Nobody in the book says
   the word "budget."
5. **They don't know they've been schooled until the glossary.** Twenty-one terms in the back,
   each page-referenced to where it actually happened. The book confesses on the last page —
   which is both the proof the smuggling worked and the best single line for a cold letter.
   The site already says it well at `index.html`: *"they're consumers now. Clueless ones, most
   likely — but consumers all the same… before they realize they've been schooled."*
