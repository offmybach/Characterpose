## The letter

Dear [Name],

You already know the quietest risk in your book of business: what happens to assets when they change generations. Ask any practice-management consultant — when the wealth moves, the heirs usually move it somewhere else. And why wouldn't they? Most have never had a single conversation with their parents' advisor. By the time you meet them, they're grown, they're grieving, and they have a college friend who "does money."

Meet them at seven instead.

I'm an attorney, an illustrator, and a dad, and I wrote *Clarence Gets a Bargain* — a 36-page picture book, ages 6–10, where one kid walks through one complete purchase: earning the reward, researching the sale ads, comparison shopping the aisle, catching the clearance markdown, redeeming a coupon, paying the sales tax, and keeping the receipt. Sixteen-plus financial concepts, hidden inside a robot story, with a 21-term glossary in the back that points each definition to the page where it happens.

And on page 6 there is something I have not found in any other picture book: a 529 plan. In the plot. Explained by a mom, eye-rolled at by a kid, and defended by a dad two scenes later. Your move.

Why this belongs in your practice:

**It opens the conversations you already want to have.** Hand the book to a client family and the 529 discussion starts itself at their kitchen counter, on their schedule, with their kid asking the questions. You didn't pitch anything. The robot did.

**It attaches you to the family's money story.** Branded golf balls get lost. Market-commentary emails get archived. The book that taught a client's kid about money — with your card inside the cover — sits on a shelf in that house for a decade and gets read at bedtime by the exact people whose names end up on the accounts.

**It starts the heir relationship two decades early.** The kid who learned *Wants vs. Needs* from the book you gave the family is the same kid who, at 27, decides whether the family's advisor is "Mom's guy" or "the person who's been part of how our family does money since I was in first grade." One of those gets fired.

**It scales past your client list.** Client-appreciation events, school sponsorships, library donations, community workshops — the book ships with a free educator toolkit (four 45-minute lesson plans, discussion guide, assessments, a standards chart mapped to Jump$tart, Common Core Math, Common Core ELA, CEE, and FDIC Money Smart), so a "money night" for client families is already built. You bring the book and the name tag; the curriculum is done.

**Your compliance officer will not blink.** It's a $19.99 children's book, not a soft-dollar problem. It is endorsed by a veteran K–5 educator with 30+ years in the classroom and by the 2026 EIFLE Educator of the Year, so it also survives the client who googles everything.

Specs, if you're the diligence type (you are): ISBN 979-8-234-07638-0, LCCN 2026906164, hardbound, full color, est. Lexile AD 620L. Full reference: www.clarencegetsabargain.com/book-facts.html. Bulk and gifting orders: orders@clarencegetsabargain.com — POs and Net 30 accepted.

Twenty-five copies run $499.75. If one of those twenty-five families opens one 529 you'd have missed — or one heir stays put in 2046 because your firm was part of the furniture — the box paid for itself several hundred times over. You can check my math; it's that kind of book.
