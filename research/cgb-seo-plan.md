# Getting CGB to Rank: What the Search Results Say

Research date: July 12, 2026. Based on 12 query sweeps across the children's /
kids' financial literacy space plus direct checks on the CGB site. Companion
spreadsheet: `finlit-search-competitors.csv`.

---

## The headline problem

**CGB doesn't rank low. It doesn't rank at all.**

Searching the exact phrase `"Clarence Gets a Bargain"` — in quotes — returns
the Cartoon Network show *Clarence*, Piggly Wiggly history, and HBO Max
episode pages. Adding "book" and "Jonathan Bach" still returns zero CGB
results. The site is live, crawlable, has a clean sitemap and robots.txt, and
has good structured data. The pages are fine. Google either hasn't indexed
them or has no reason to surface them.

Fix indexing and the brand collision before spending a minute on keyword
strategy. A site that can't win its own name can't win "financial literacy
for kids."

---

## Who owns page one, and why

Four tiers showed up across every query. Full detail in the spreadsheet.

**Tier 1 — Government and regulators (unbeatable, but useful).**
FDIC Money Smart appeared in 6 of 12 searches. CFPB, FINRA, Cleveland Fed,
state treasurers. They rank because .gov authority plus free curriculum
equals thousands of school and bank backlinks. You will not outrank them.
You don't need to — see "ride the lists" below.

**Tier 2 — Banks and brokerages doing content marketing.**
Schwab, Fidelity, Bank of America, Morgan Stanley, Eastspring. They rank on
raw domain authority plus permanent topic hubs (Schwab runs an entire
separate site, schwabmoneywise.com). But note the small fry in this tier:
Southern Bank and Stillman Bank — regional banks — rank on page one with a
single evergreen post. The bar on these terms is lower than it looks.

**Tier 3 — Niche players who picked one phrase and own it.**
MoneyTime (moneytimekids.com) ranks for "financial literacy course for kids"
because that's literally its homepage title and domain. Fin Lit Kids owns
"finlit kids" the same way, and stacks page one with its Shopify store,
YouTube channel, Instagram, and press hits. NFEC does it with
financialeducatorscouncil.org. Lesson: exact-match focus beats general
authority on narrow phrases.

**Tier 4 — The listicle layer (this is where books get bought).**
Every book-intent query ("financial literacy books for kids," "children's
books about money") is dominated by roundups: U.S. News "Best Money Books
for Kids," A Mighty Girl, Self Sufficient Kids (28 books), My First Nest
Egg, Lucky Little Learners, ABA Foundation's book list, ALA LibGuides,
Missouri State Treasurer's book page, bank blogs. Plus Amazon and
Barnes & Noble category pages. **No individual book site ranks for these
queries. The books that win are ON the lists, not competing with them.**

## The plan, in priority order

### 1. Fix indexing (this week, free)

- Verify the domain in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
  Submit the sitemap. Request indexing on `/`, `book-facts.html`,
  `educator-toolkit.html`, and the resource pages.
- Check Search Console's coverage report for why pages aren't indexed.
  A brand-new domain with near-zero inbound links often sits in the
  "discovered, not crawled" bucket for months. Backlinks (step 3) are the
  cure.

### 2. Win the brand query (this month)

The cartoon owns "Clarence." Every page title, heading, and schema entry
should disambiguate: **"Clarence Gets a Bargain — Children's Book"** or
"…Picture Book by Jonathan Bach." The current homepage title ("Story First.
Money Smarts Sneak In.") is great copy but gives Google nothing to
distinguish the book from the show. Add "children's book" and "Jonathan
Bach" to the title tag; keep the tagline after it.

Also claim every free authority profile that ranks instantly for book
titles: **Amazon author page, Goodreads book + author page, Google Books,
BookBub**. These outrank a new site for its own title from day one and
funnel to the site.

### 3. Ride the lists instead of fighting them (ongoing, highest ROI)

The book-buying queries are owned by roundups. Getting CGB onto them does
two jobs at once: puts the book where buyers already look AND builds the
backlinks the domain needs to rank on its own. Pitch, roughly in order of
attainability:

- **State treasurer book lists.** Missouri's page ranks nationally; the
  state-of-the-states page already maps who runs finlit programs. Every
  state treasurer with a reading list is a .gov backlink.
- **ABA Foundation Financial Literacy Book List** — bankers hand this list
  to schools.
- **ALA LibGuides / Thinking Money for Kids** — librarians are already a
  target audience, and the press kit + book-facts page exist for exactly
  this pitch.
- **Blog roundups**: Self Sufficient Kids, A Mighty Girl, My First Nest Egg,
  Lucky Little Learners, Helping Kids Rise. These update their lists;
  a review copy plus the educator toolkit is the pitch.
- **U.S. News "Best Money Books for Kids"** — harder, but one placement
  drives more sales than a #1 ranking on any keyword here.
- **Bank and credit union blogs.** They demonstrably rank with one post,
  and they're already CGB's institutional-buyer target. Every bulk-order
  partnership should include a link: "We give Clarence Gets a Bargain to
  local classrooms" with a link is CRA-friendly and SEO gold. The
  grant-in-a-box CRA memo already sets this up — add a link ask to it.

### 4. Give each keyword a real page (next 1–2 months)

A single-page site can't rank for a dozen different intents. The resource
pages exist but most content is locked inside canvas drawings, modals, and
print-HTML — invisible to crawlers. Build a small set of indexable,
text-first pages, each targeting one proven query from the research:

| Target query | Page |
|---|---|
| financial literacy books for kids / children's books about money | "Why kids' money books fail (and what a story can do instead)" — positions CGB inside the genre buyers are searching |
| teaching kids about money | Age-by-age guide in CGB voice, drawing on the book's concepts (the age-by-age format ranked for two regional banks) |
| financial literacy curriculum elementary | Indexable landing page for the educator toolkit — lesson plans, standards, all 5 frameworks, in crawlable text |
| clearance / markdown / comparison shopping for kids | The 21-term glossary as a real HTML page, one anchor per term — definition queries are low competition and the glossary already exists verbatim |
| wants vs needs for kids | wants-vs-needs.html already exists — give it a text intro, title tag, and internal links |

Rules: real HTML text (not canvas), one intent per page, internal links
between them, Book + FAQ schema where it fits. Respect the spoiler rules —
the Clarence/Clearance reveal stays off all public pages.

### 5. Skip the "finlit" keyword (decision, zero effort)

"Children's finlit" and "kids finlit" are brand-name turf (Fin Lit Kids,
FinLit Fest, Finlit App) with tiny search volume. The buyers — parents,
teachers, librarians — search "money books for kids" and "teaching kids
about money." Spend nothing here.

### 6. Second channel: YouTube (when there's time)

Fin Lit Kids takes multiple page-one slots through its YouTube channel
alone. Video results rank on these queries with far less competition than
web pages. A book trailer, a read-aloud sample (first few pages only), or
"the shopping homework game" as a 3-minute video would give CGB entries in
a lane the site can't reach yet.

---

## What NOT to worry about

- **On-page SEO of the current site.** Meta tags, schema, sitemap, llms.txt
  are already in decent shape. On-page polish is not the constraint;
  authority and indexable text are.
- **Outranking FDIC/Schwab/Ramsey on head terms.** Never happening, never
  necessary. The buyers on those pages get caught via the lists in step 3.
- **Paid search.** Nothing here requires it; the organic gaps are cheap to
  fill first.

## Measuring it

Search Console after step 1 gives the baseline (likely near zero). Track:
indexed pages, impressions for "clarence gets a bargain book," referring
domains (target: +1 per month from step 3), and clicks on book-intent
queries once step 4 pages go live.
