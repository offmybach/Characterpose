✂ Run of Show

What Happens During a Visit.

Roughly 45 minutes. No prep required from teachers. Here's how it flows.

01

The Setup

Jonathan introduces the book and Clarence's mission — earn a robot reward, then figure out how to spend it like a smart shopper.

02

The Read-Aloud

Full read-through with stops for interactive questions. Kids predict what Clarence should do next. Spoiler: they argue about it.

03

The Shopping Game

Students compare two robots side-by-side — price, features, markdown. They vote. They debate. One kid always argues for the more expensive one. He's usually wrong.

04

Q&A + Concepts

Open Q&A covers the financial terms from the story — budgeting, comparison shopping, coupons, clearance, savings. Teachers get a terms reference sheet.

05

The Signing

If books are available, Jonathan signs. For assemblies, a signing table is set up after dismissal. Personalized inscriptions — just bring the book.

✂ Funding Your Visit

How Schools Pay for It.

Most schools find at least one budget line that fits. Here are the most common ones.

PTA / PTO Budget

Author visits are a standard PTA line item. If your PTA has done any cultural arts event this year, this qualifies. A one-page visit description is available on request.

Title I Funds

Financial literacy author events are eligible under Title I supplemental services. Check with your school's Title I coordinator — most already have an author visit category.

Literacy Grants

Dollar General, Barnes & Noble, and state humanities councils (Maryland Humanities, Delaware Humanities) all fund school author events. Grant cycles vary; plan 60–90 days ahead.

Credit Union Partnership

Maryland and Delaware credit unions actively fund K–5 financial literacy programming. If your district has a credit union relationship, this is the fastest path to full sponsorship.

Split Across Classrooms

For multi-classroom visits, the fee splits across participating classes. A visit covering four classrooms often costs less per class than a single field trip bus.

Cultural Arts Programming

Many districts have a cultural arts or enrichment fund separate from classroom budgets. Check with your arts or enrichment coordinator — author visits are a natural fit.

Documentation for grant applications and PTA budget requests is available on request. Happy to write a one-paragraph program description for your proposal.

✂ Logistics

The Practical Stuff.

Most schools have the same questions. Here they are.

How far in advance should we book?

4–6 weeks is ideal. Fall slots (September–November) fill early — if you're planning a financial literacy month event in October, reach out in August.

What does the school need to provide?

Projector or display screen for the virtual option. For in-person: a microphone if the group is larger than 80 students. That's it. No prep work, no printed materials needed in advance.

Are books available for purchase on the day?

Yes — order forms go home two weeks before the visit so families can pre-order. Jonathan signs every copy, including those students already own. Hardbound, $19.99.

Can teachers use the free educator resources before the visit?

Absolutely — and it makes the visit land harder when kids already know the characters. All six classroom resources are free at clarencegetsabargain.com/educator-toolkit. No email required.

What grade levels is this right for?

Written for Grades 1–5. The read-aloud and shopping game are designed so younger students engage with the story and older students dig into the financial concepts. Mixed-grade assemblies work well.

Is there a fee?

Yes. Pricing is based on visit format and travel. Email to discuss — the goal is to make the visit work within your budget, not the other way around. Virtual visits are priced lower than in-person.

Reserve Your Date.

Slots are limited. Email to check availability — include your school name, grade levels, preferred format, and a rough date range.
